Is Facebook Declining?

By Kazi MD Arafat Rahaman  ·  Written for Business Inspection BD, published under a different byline
Table of Contents
  1. Overview
  2. Why Declining

With nearly 3 billion users, Facebook is the world's largest social media platform. At its highest peak, the company had a market capitalization of over $1(1.07) trillion. But on February 2, 2022, Facebook's parent company, Mate’s share price dropped by 26.4 percent, which continued to reduce. On October 12, 2022,  its market cap stood at $345.46 billion. And as the market capitalization of Matter has decreased, so has Mark Zuckerberg's personal wealth. Zuckerberg's net worth was $125 billion in January 2022, but his current net worth is down by 61 percent to $48.4 billion, Bloomberg reports. In addition to the Cambridge Analytica scandal and the legal battle with the US Congress over the past few years, Facebook's monthly average users and revenue also fell in the second quarter of 2022.

Overview

In just three years after its launch in 2004, 50 million users signed up on the social website in October 2007. After five years, Facebook became the world's most popular social platform, with 350 million registered users and 132 million monthly visitors in 2009. In April 2012, Facebook acquired Instagram for $1 billion, and on May 18 of the same year, the company got listed as IPO. At the time, Facebook was the third-largest US tech-based company with a valuation of $104 billion. Later in October 2021, Facebook was rebranded as Meta, where Meta is essentially the parent company that includes several other brands like Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and WhatsApp. Instead of just managing social networks, the company is now working on virtual and augmented reality, known as Metaverse. According to Mark Zuckerberg, Metaverse will be a virtual world where all users will feel connected with more social technologies.

Just one year after launching Metaverse, the company lost 1 million users for the first time in the 4th quarter of 2021. However, it increased again in subsequent quarters. However, in the 2nd quarter of 2022, the number of monthly average users has decreased by 2 billion. Mater's annual report shows that the number of Facebook users decreased in Africa and Latin American countries this quarter. In the first two quarters of 2022, Facebook's daily active users in the European region also fell by 6 million. In addition to the user decline, the company's revenue in the second quarter of 2022 was down about 1 percent from the same quarter a year ago. 97 percent of Meta's total earnings come from advertising revenue. The company generated $28.15 billion in revenue in the second quarter of 2022, which was about 1.5 percent lower than the same quarter a year ago. Again, the company is constantly losing from the Reality Labs division i.e. Metaverse. During the first quarter of 2022, Reality Lab lost about $3(2.96) billion, and in the second quarter, it lost $2.8 billion. Earlier in 2021, Metaverse lost $10.2 billion. Since the launch of the Metaverse, the total loss in this sector has been $18.05 billion.

Why Declining

Lack of Growth Opportunity

Today, the world population is about 8 billion, of which more than 5 billion people have access to the Internet. Meanwhile, Facebook has about 3 billion users worldwide. The remaining 5 billion include 1.4 billion people in China, 83 million in Iran, and 25 million in North Korea who cannot directly use Facebook because the governments of these countries have banned Facebook. Thus, most internet users around the world already use Facebook. Due to this, Facebook's user growth rate has decreased over time. For example, from the second quarter of 2020 to the second quarter of 2022, the number of Facebook users increased by only 12 percent.

Rise of Competitive Social Media

Facebook's slowing growth is also due to the rise of other social media sites that compete with it. Twitter's user base grew by 43.25 percent, from 166 million in the first quarter of 2020 to nearly 238 million in the second quarter of 2022. These users include politicians, celebrities, and people from all walks of life. LinkedIn, a social media site for professionals, had 9.57 percent more users from 2020 to 2021. In the fourth quarter of 2021, there were 756 million users on the site.

On the other hand, YouTube has the most users of all video-sharing sites. Between 2020 and 2021, YouTube users grew by 13 percent to 2.6 billion. Facebook's biggest rival right now is the vertical video sharing platform TikTok, which is also popular with Millennials and Gen Z. This Chinese platform for sharing short-term videos has more than 1 billion active users each month. From 347 million users in the first quarter of 2020 to 1.64 billion users in the second quarter of 2022, TikTok users grew by 373.5%. From 229 million users in the first quarter of 2020 to 347 million users in the second quarter of 2022, another popular social platform like Snapchat grew by almost 52.53 percent. As the number of people using these platforms grows, Facebook's growth rate also slows down.

Privacy Concern of User Data

97 percent of Facebook or Mater's total earnings come from advertising revenue. However, the company's revenue declined for the first time in the second quarter of 2022, and analysts, including Facebook, blamed Apple's privacy-centric updates for this. In the 3rd quarter of 2021, Apple changed its privacy policy and implemented the app tracking transparency feature. The specialty of this feature is that if an app wants to track the user's activity on the Apple device, the app must take permission from the user. Naturally, no user wants his personal data or interests to be known by anyone else. This new feature has affected Facebook's ad revenue since it earns almost all of its revenue by giving targeted ads based on this data. According to a source from CNBC, the new privacy policy has resulted in a $10 billion loss in Facebook's ad revenue so far in 2022. However, Sheryl Sandberg, chief operating officer and architect of Facebook's marketing strategy, told the 2021 Q4 investor meeting that Facebook has ways to do targeted advertising with fewer data. With the company's targeting and measurement tools in place, any business can benefit, no matter how big or small. However, privacy concerns among users are constantly increasing, and various operating systems and web browsers are constantly introducing privacy-centric features for users, which can hurt Facebook's ability to deliver targeted ads.

Lack Of Innovation

Since the middle of the last decade, Facebook has needed help coming up with new ideas. Since Facebooks Inception, apart from Facebook and Messenger, the company didn't launch any new products. At this time, Facebook was more interested in buying up other social media apps that were competing with it. For example, in 2012, Facebook bought Instagram, and in 2014, it bought WhatsApp. Mark Zuckerberg tried to buy Snapchat for $3 billion in 2013 but failed. Instead, he copied Snapchat's "Stories" feature and put it in Facebook's other apps. Again, as TikTok's popularity grew, the company released a Reels feature similar to TikTok. As the company adds more and more features to its apps, they become less stable. Users are also becoming more interested in other social media platforms because the company's existing apps, which have been around for a long time, aren't giving them the smooth, bug-free experience they expect. Aside from this, Facebook announced the launch of other features or services, such as gaming apps and cryptocurrency, but later stopped working on them.

Controversy

Maintaining user privacy is one of the main concerns for any social media platform, and the debate on this matter does not seem to be leaving behind Facebook. In 2018, Mark Zuckerberg had to face Congressional hearings several times in the wake of the Cambridge Analytica scandal in which Facebook created voter profiles from 87 million user data. Mark Zuckerberg's legal battle with senators went viral online, and general users and governments worldwide became more concerned about user privacy. In addition, space X and Tesla founder Elon Musk announced removing his personal and company profiles from Facebook, which negatively affected Facebook's popularity.

Economic Recession

Another reason behind Facebook's revenue decline could be the economic slowdown caused by the global pandemic and the Russia-Ukraine wire. In addition, every country's currency has depreciated against the dollar due to the ongoing economic recession, and global inflation has increased. Therefore, with the increase in the price of goods throughout the world, the purchasing power of the common people is also decreasing, resulting in gradually fewer purchases. Therefore, economists and financial analysts from major financial firms predict that the entire period of 2023 will be a tough time for the global economy. As a result, marketers, and brands can also reduce the number of ads, ultimately affecting Facebook's revenue.

Betting on MetaVerse

Combining the physical and digital worlds, Facebook is creating the Metaverse using virtual and augmented reality. The company estimates that Metaverse will be accessible to people by 2030. However, investors are still determining whether Zuckerberg's massive investment in creating Metaverse will attract enough users. In 2021 alone, Meta invested 10 billion dollars in the Metaverse project because, according to Mark Zuckerberg, the Metaverse is the next big thing. Since its launch in 2020, Facebook and Meta's Reality Labs or Metaverse division has been losing money for seven consecutive quarters, despite generating revenue. Due to this, the company's profit has also decreased and the investors' confidence in the company has reduced and as a result, the company's share price has also decreased.
