The 263 million people of the [Asia and the Pacific region](https://www.adb.org/who-we-are/main#:~:text=Despite%20the%20region%27s,and%20economic%20development.), known as the fastest growing regions in the world, have an average income of less than 2 dollars, and more than 1 billion people still have an income of less than 3.20 dollars. By developing infrastructural assets in Asia and the Pacific region for the benefit of socio-economic welfare of their citizens as well as achieving a prosperous and sustainable region for their communities, the Asian Development Bank or ADB works to alleviate poverty. Similar to the World Bank, the ADB works to alleviate poverty in Asia and the Pacific, but its activities are limited to that region.

Asian Development Bank, or ADB, mainly deals with the social and economic development of Asia and the Pacific region. [In 1956](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/235061/adb-history-book-second-edition.pdf), Japanese Finance Minister Hisato Ichimada, expressed his interest in establishing a financial institution to manage various development activities in the Asian region to the US Secretary of State John Foster Dulles. A year later, the Japanese Prime Minister at that time, Nobusuke Kishi announced that Japan, in cooperation with other industrially developed countries, wanted to establish a Regional Development Fund from which will manage development activities across Southeast Asia. But because America did not respond to this initiative at that time, other economically developed countries also did not show much interest in this matter.

On the other hand, in 1962, an economist named Kaoru Ohashi met a privet financial consultant named Takeshi Watanabe and discussed establishing a development group for the Asian region. They both did viability research on this project for one year and approached to World Bank, but the World Bank eventually rejected this project. Later, this proposal was again raised in a session of the United Nations' Economic Commission for Asia and the Far East (ECAFE) in 1963, and there, for the first time, the decision was taken to establish such a financial institution. Takeshi Watanabe's previous research was used in this project, and since then, Japan and ECAFE have decided to work together. At that time, America didnt deny the project but did not come forward to help. Later, Manila, Philippines, was selected to set up the headquarters of the organization through 3 rounds of voting with 18 members. The first meeting of the Asian Development Bank was held on 24 November 1966, where Watanabe was elected as the first president of the ADB. With 31 member countries, the Asian Development Bank was founded on 19th December of that year. Later in 1974, [Asian Development Fund (ADF)](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/funds/adf/overview) was established for the proper management of ADB funds. Although initially, the sole function of ADF was to disburse loans in view of low interest, since 2017, ADF has been functioning solely as a grants facility.

[ADB's activities](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/development-issues) include agriculture and mineral resources, transport and communication, digital technology, energy, industry, finance, and social infrastructure development. The organization currently has [68 countries](https://www.adb.org/who-we-are/about#accordion-1-0:~:text=ADB%20By%2DLaws-,Members,-From%2031%20members) as members, of which 49 are in the Asia and Pacific region and the remaining 19 are non-regional countries such as the USA, the UK and various European countries. ADB has a Governor and an Alternate Governor from each member country. There are [136 governors](https://www.adb.org/who-we-are/organization/board-governors) from 68 countries who select the [12 members of the Board of Directors](https://www.adb.org/who-we-are/organization/board-directors), including eight regional members and four non-regional members. The bank president is elected from this board of directors. The country with higher economic contribution gets higher voting rights while electing the president. Since its inception, all ADB presidents have been Japanese because Japan has made the highest [financial contribution](https://data.adb.org/media/7886/download) to ADB every year (15.75 percent). America has the same amount of economic assistance and voting power after Japan (12.75 percent). Other countries holding top voting power include China (5.437 percent), India (5.437 percent), and Canada (4.469 percent).

[Bangladesh](https://www.adb.org/countries/bangladesh/main) joined ADB as a member country in 1973. So far, Bangladesh has received more than $28 billion in project support and technical assistance in 811 projects in various sectors, including IT, education, transport, energy, agriculture, and public sector management. Bangladesh has 1.109 percent voting power in ADB.

Also, based on the needs of developing member countries, ADB provides various types of financial support, which plays an important role in boosting the country's economic growth besides helping the governments of the member countries to face various development challenges. In addition, ADB provides technical assistance such as project viability checking and research assistance support to various infrastructure development projects of ADB member countries.

## Technical Assistance

ADB provides two types of [technical assistance](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#:~:text=Technical%20assistance%20%28TA%29) to its member countries in various development activities. ADB's expert team offers Transaction Based Technical Assistance, which involves calculating risk factors for self-funded projects, implementing public-private partnership projects through pilot testing, and providing policy advice and transaction advisory services. On the other hand, in projects that are not funded by ADB, they provide knowledge and support-based technical assistance through sector-based economic studies, research and development of projects, including sector strategy building, investment master plans, making pipelines for future projects, and capacity building.

## Financing

Apart from technical assistance, ADB provides three types of financial support in the public and private sectors and those are - investment landing, policy-based landing and grants.

### Investment Lending

Under this type of lending, ADB gives its member countries five different kinds of loans. [Project loans](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#accordion-0-0:~:text=to%20specific%20projects.-,Project%20Loan,-ADB%E2%80%99s%20most%20commonly) are the most common way for the ADB to help member countries to improve their infrastructure. ADB member countries can use this loan to do specific development work in their sectors, like energy, transportation, urban and water, education, health, public sector management and integration. ADB also gives loans, called [sector loans](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#accordion-0-0:~:text=Sovereign%20Operations%20%282017%29-,Sector%20Loan,-Finances%20numerous%2C%20smaller), for smaller projects that are part of these infrastructure development projects. The loan amount for these kinds of projects varies based on the situation and the project's importance.

Again, the ADB helps keep the economy stable by giving loans to business sectors in a country. These are called "[Financial Intermediate Loans](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#accordion-0-0:~:text=Financial%20Intermediation%20Loan%20%28FIL%29)." The ADB lends money directly to a country's government in this case. The country's government then returns the money to banks and other organizations. Later, these institutions give small loans to micro, small, and medium-sized businesses, women entrepreneurs, and low-income groups in the country. ADB also offers loans to help people get back on their feet quickly after natural disasters like earthquakes, storms, floods, etc. Also, the ADB gives out "[multi-tranche financing facilities](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#accordion-0-0:~:text=Multitranche%20Financing%20Facility%20%28MFF%29)," which are large loans with long-term commitments to complex projects.

### Policy-Based Lending

If a country's annual budget has a shortfall, the ADB finds extra money to make up for it. However, the borrowing government can only use the funds for General Development Expenditures. This kind of funding is called [Policy Based Funding or PBL](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#:~:text=Policy%2DBased%20Lending%20%28PBL%29). If this kind of loan is approved, the borrower has to sign a Memorandum of Understanding with the ADB. This document has several conditions, such as improving the way projects are paid for with loan money collect money, making sure that public resources are managed well, and making the business environment friendlier for investors.

Again, ADB loans combine PBL and investment loans for various [sector development programs](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#:~:text=Sector%20Development%20Program%20%28SDP%29) or SDPs. This is done when a country needs money for an infrastructure development project that also requires policy changes in that sector. ADB also gives [project readiness financing](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#:~:text=Project%20Readiness%20Financing%20%28PRF%29) for checks on the project's viability and consulting services like engineering design, capacity building, limited help with project start-up, and pilot testing of project designs.

Also, the ADB offers [Result-Based Loans (RBL)](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#:~:text=Results%2DBased%20Lending%20%28RBL%29) for a country's internal financial management, procurement, safeguards, monitoring, and evaluation. If ADB-funded projects need a small amount of money for activities like maintenance, rehabilitation, etc. that will affect the project's overall outcome, the institution will lend the money through the [Small Expenditure Financing Facility (SEFF)](https://www.adb.org/what-we-do/public-sector-financing/financial-products-modalities#:~:text=Small%20Expenditure%20Financing%20Facility%20%28SEFF%29) program.

### Grants

To raise the living standard of the poorest and most vulnerable countries, ADF provides [grants support](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/535676/together-we-deliver-2019.pdf) to member countries that are still having trouble with climate change, gender inequality, poor infrastructure, limited private sector development, and reducing poverty. Basically, this grant is given to promote gender equality, help countries that have been torn apart by war, improve governance and capacity, raise awareness about climate change and disasters, and build shelters. For example, in January 2022, the UN gave war-torn Afghanistan a [$405 million](https://www.adb.org/news/405-million-adb-grants-support-food-security-health-education-afghanistan-united-nations) grant to help improve food security, health care, and education. In April of the same year, Samoa was given a grant of [$10 million](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-provides-10-million-grant-help-samoa-manage-covid-19) to help solve the Covid 19 problem.
